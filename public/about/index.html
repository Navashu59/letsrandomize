About LetsRandomize

What LetsRandomize does

LetsRandomize publishes free browser-based tools for shuffling lists, drawing numbers, forming teams, generating prompts, and making low-stakes random choices. The goal is practical: open a page, enter only what the tool needs, and get a result without creating an account.

Sam Parker writes and maintains the tool explanations, guides, and operating policies. Each page is intended to describe the tool that is actually available, including limits that may affect how a result should be used.

How the randomizers work

The shared randomization engine uses the browser Web Crypto API when it is available and falls back to Math.random in older environments. A result is selected at the time you use the tool. Some tools draw independently on every click; others, such as the card picker in no-replacement mode, keep a temporary deck state until you reset it or reload the page.

Random selection can remove a person's preference from a low-stakes choice, but it cannot make an unsuitable option safe or fair. Check the available choices before generating and use judgment whenever a result affects another person.

How tools and content are checked

Interactive pages are tested for their main input, generate, copy, share, reset, and history flows where those controls are present. Automated checks look for missing authorship, repeated boilerplate, unsupported privacy claims, thin pages, and problems in the built-in word and prompt libraries.

Sam Parker checks factual claims against the code and interface, removes unsupported statements, and distinguishes random selection from decisions that require judgment. Pages are updated when a tool changes or when search and user data reveal a missing explanation. Corrections are made in the page and recorded with an updated review date.

Prompt and word-list policy

Built-in prompts are written for general use and reviewed to remove sexual, discriminatory, humiliating, dangerous, or needlessly invasive material. Category labels help narrow a list, but they do not certify every prompt for every age, classroom, workplace, family, or culture. A facilitator should preview a result and make passing easy.

Word generators use curated English lists for brainstorming and informal language activities. They are not dictionaries, curriculum lists, or guarantees that a word has only one part of speech. When spelling, meaning, or suitability matters, check an appropriate reference.

Privacy and appropriate use

User-entered values and generated results are processed by the tool in your browser. Recent-result features may use browser storage. Hosting, analytics, advertising, and security providers may still receive technical request data; the Privacy Policy explains the services used by the site.

Random tools are appropriate only when every possible result is acceptable. They should not decide medical, legal, financial, safety, consent, grading, hiring, discipline, or other high-impact matters.

Corrections and contact

To report a broken tool, inaccurate statement, unsuitable built-in prompt, or accessibility problem, email navashu72@gmail.com. Include the page address, what you expected, and what happened. Reports that identify a specific result or browser are especially useful.
