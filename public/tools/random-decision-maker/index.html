Random Decision Maker

Enter at least two options, one per line, then make a neutral random pick.

Recent Results

How do I make a random decision?

Replace the example text with at least two options, putting one option on each line. Remove anything you would not actually accept, then click Choose an Option. Blank lines are ignored and the selected line appears below the button.

A random decision maker is most useful after you have narrowed the choice to acceptable alternatives. It can settle low-stakes ties such as which meal to cook, which reviewed activity to do first, who takes the first turn, or which task to start when order does not matter.

How are the odds calculated?

Every non-empty line is one entry. If four different options appear once, each receives one of four available positions. The tool selects one position at random. Leading or trailing blank lines do not create extra options.

Duplicate lines do count separately. If "Walk" appears twice and "Movie" appears once, Walk occupies two of the three positions. That gives it more weight. Remove accidental duplicates when you want equal treatment.

Can I create a weighted random choice?

Yes, by repeating an option. To give one choice approximately twice the weight of another, enter it on two lines while the other appears once. For a 3:2:1 ratio, enter the options three times, twice, and once.

This simple method works for small casual lists. Check the entries before generating because spelling variants such as "Takeout" and "take out" are treated as separate lines. For regulated or high-impact selection, use a system that records and validates weights explicitly.

What are good uses for a random choice picker?

Use it for choices where preference is weak or the group has already agreed that every result is fair. Examples include selecting a warm-up activity, choosing one of several approved discussion questions, rotating household chores, picking a restaurant from a shortlist, or deciding which creative prompt to attempt.

For a group, show the final input list before choosing so everyone can spot omissions or duplicates. Agree whether the result is final, whether one redraw is allowed, and what happens if circumstances make the chosen option unavailable.

When should I not decide randomly?

Do not delegate medical, legal, financial, safety, consent, disciplinary, hiring, grading, or other high-impact decisions to a random picker. These decisions require evidence, responsibility, and consideration of consequences. A random result cannot make an unsuitable option appropriate.

If one choice feels unacceptable after it wins, that reaction is information. Remove it, reconsider the criteria, or make the decision deliberately. Random selection is a tie-breaker for acceptable options, not a replacement for judgment.

Is this different from a decision wheel?

The underlying job is similar: choose one option from a list. A wheel provides a visual spin that may be better for a live group, while this page provides a compact text input and result. Use Spin the Wheel when the animation matters, or the Yes or No Generator for a simple binary choice.
