Recent Results

How do I draw a random playing card?

Choose how many cards to draw, whether cards return to the deck, and whether to include jokers. Click Generate to see each rank and suit, then use Copy or Share when you want to keep the result.

The deck contains ace through king in clubs, diamonds, hearts, and spades. You may add two jokers, draw up to 13 cards at once, and choose whether cards return to the deck. The picker does not assign a game-specific value to the ace; treat it as high or low according to your activity.

Does the card return to the deck?

Only when Return cards to the deck is selected. With replacement, the same card can appear more than once. Without replacement, drawn cards stay out until Reset Deck is used. Each named card has a 1 in 52 chance on each draw, each suit has a 1 in 4 chance, and each rank has a 1 in 13 chance.

Replacement mode is useful for independent prompts or probability examples. Leave replacement off when you want to deal from one shuffled deck and see the remaining-card count after each draw.

Can I draw multiple cards without replacement?

Yes. Choose a count from two to 13 and leave Return cards to the deck off to draw a hand without duplicates. The remaining-card counter updates after every draw. Use Reset Deck to restore and reshuffle the full deck.

Switching on replacement makes each card an independent pick and allows duplicates. Keep it off for hands, turn order, or any activity where a card should not reappear before the reset.

What can I use a random card for?

Use one card as a writing or drawing prompt, a quick game input, a classroom probability example, or a way to select one of 52 preassigned options. For a writing exercise, suits might represent settings and ranks might represent characters or events. Define the mapping before drawing so the result is not adjusted afterward.

For exercise or study routines, assign a task type to each suit and a repetition or question number to each rank. Review the activity first and make sure every possible card leads to an appropriate task.

How do card probabilities work?

In a standard deck, there are 26 red cards, 26 black cards, 13 cards in each suit, four cards of each rank, and 12 face cards if jacks, queens, and kings are counted. With replacement, those probabilities stay the same on every click.

Without replacement, probabilities change after each card. If the first card is a heart and is kept out, only 12 hearts remain among 51 cards. In no-replacement mode the remaining-card count reflects that change. In replacement mode the chance of a heart returns to 13 out of 52 for every pick.

When should I use another randomizer?

Use the Random Number Generator when you need a custom numeric range, the Coin Flip Simulator for two equally likely outcomes, or the Random Decision Maker when your options are words rather than playing cards.

This card picker is intended for casual activities and demonstrations. It is not a certified system for gambling, regulated games, or high-value drawings.
