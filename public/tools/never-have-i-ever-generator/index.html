How do I use the Never Have I Ever generator?

Click Generate to select one statement from the built-in general-audience list. Read it before sharing it with the group and generate again when it does not fit the setting. You can copy or share the current result, and recent results remain available in the browser history panel until they are cleared.

The page does not have adult, spicy, family, school, or workplace category filters. It also does not accept custom statements. The list is deliberately broad, so the person leading the activity still needs to decide whether each prompt is suitable.

How do you play Never Have I Ever?

Choose a response method before the first prompt. In the hand version, everyone begins with a fixed number of fingers raised and lowers one after a statement applies to them. In a no-score version, participants simply raise a hand, move to one side of the room, or say whether the statement applies.

Agree on the end condition and pass rule in advance. A short round might use ten prompts, while a conversation round may stop whenever one statement leads to a good voluntary story. Nobody should have to explain an answer.

What makes a good Never Have I Ever question?

A useful statement is easy to understand, gives people room to answer without revealing sensitive information, and matches the relationship between participants. Everyday experiences, hobbies, travel, food, harmless mistakes, and imaginative situations usually create more comfortable conversation than prompts about health, finances, relationships, illegal behavior, or trauma.

Read the room as well as the words. A question that works among close friends may be unsuitable at work, in a classroom, or with people who have just met. Re-rolling is part of using a mixed prompt list responsibly.

Can I use it for kids, classrooms, or work?

The prompts are intended for a broad audience, but the list is not certified for every age or organization. An adult should preview each statement before using it with children or students. In a classroom, use the activity for optional warm-ups or language practice, not for grading participation or asking students to disclose personal experiences.

For work groups, keep responses low-pressure and avoid statements involving protected characteristics, health, family status, compensation, religion, politics, or conduct that could create embarrassment. A neutral Random Question Generator may be a better choice when the group does not know one another well.

How do we keep the game comfortable?

Make three rules explicit: anyone may pass, nobody has to explain, and the group may skip a prompt without debate. Do not use a response to tease, pressure, expose, or judge another participant. Stop the activity if someone appears uncomfortable.

Avoid using drinks, dares, penalties, or public scoring as pressure to answer. The game works when participation is voluntary and every person can leave a question unanswered without becoming the focus of attention.

Will statements repeat?

They can. Each click makes an independent random selection from the same built-in list. The history panel helps you notice recent repeats, but it does not remove previously shown prompts from future draws. Generate again when a duplicate appears.
