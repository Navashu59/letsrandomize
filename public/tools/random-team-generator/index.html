How do I split a list into random teams?

Start with the clean list, not the team settings. Put one person on each line, remove anyone who is absent, and add last initials when two people share the same first name. Then choose the number of teams you need and generate all teams at once.

If your goal is a specific group size, work backward from the roster count. For 24 people in groups of 4, choose 6 teams. For 25 people in groups of 4, choose 6 teams if one group of 5 is acceptable, or choose 7 teams if smaller groups are better for the activity.

What if I need random pairs or groups of three?

For pairs, choose enough teams so each team has two people. For example, 20 students need 10 pairs. If the current menu does not show that many teams, use the list randomizer first, then read the shuffled list in pairs or groups of three.

When one person is left over, decide the rule before generating: one group of three, a rotating helper, or a manual assignment to the smallest group. That keeps the result easy to accept and avoids rerolling until the output looks convenient.

How should I handle duplicate names before making groups?

The generator treats every line as a separate person. If two entries say "Alex", both will be included. Add a last initial, number, or role label before generating so the copied team list is clear. If a duplicate line is accidental, remove it before clicking Generate.

Are random teams the same as balanced teams?

Random teams are neutral because every name is shuffled before assignment. They are also even by size as much as the roster allows. That does not automatically mean the teams are equal by skill, age, position, or experience.

If competitive balance matters, separate known strong players or position anchors first, then randomize everyone else. If speed and social neutrality matter more, paste the full list and let the generator split the group.

The tool runs in the browser and treats each line as one entry. For more detail on the shuffle method and privacy limits, see the methodology page.

How Snake Draft Order Works

Snake draft (serpentine draft) is a useful option for balanced team selection when people already agree on rough skill order. Unlike straight drafts where Team 1 always picks first, snake draft reverses the order each round:

RoundPick 1Pick 2Pick 3Pick 4
Round 1Team ATeam BTeam CTeam D
Round 2Team DTeam CTeam BTeam A
Round 3Team ATeam BTeam CTeam D
Round 4Team DTeam CTeam BTeam A

This gives Team A the #1 and #8 players, while Team D gets #4 and #5, which usually produces a closer spread of estimated skill than a straight draft. Use it when people already agree on rough skill order; use a pure random split when speed and neutrality matter more than competitive balance.

Tips for Making Teams Fair

Random selection is the simplest fair method, but these strategies help when skill balance matters:

  • Rate players 1-10 before splitting — Have a neutral person rate each player's skill. Then distribute so each team's total rating is similar.
  • Use snake draft for competitive games — When fairness really matters (league play, tournaments), snake draft produces the most balanced teams.
  • Separate known strong players first — Place the top 2-3 players on different teams, then randomly distribute the rest.
  • Rotate teams between rounds — For ongoing events, re-shuffle teams every few games so one team doesn't dominate all day.
  • Let teams self-balance with trades — After random generation, allow a 2-minute trade window where teams can swap one player each.
  • Use position-based splitting — For sports requiring specific positions, ensure each team gets at least one player per key position (e.g., one goalkeeper per soccer team).

Team Generator for Sports and Pickup Games

When skill levels vary widely in a pickup game, pure random teams can feel unfair. Here is a quick method to balance teams while keeping it fast:

  1. Identify the top 2-4 players — everyone usually knows who they are
  2. Assign one strong player to each team manually
  3. Enter all remaining players into the generator and distribute randomly
  4. Play one game, then evaluate — if one team dominates, swap one mid-skill player and replay
