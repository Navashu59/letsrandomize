How do I generate a random question?

Click Generate to select one general-audience question from the built-in list. Generate again when the first question does not suit the moment. Use Copy or Share for the current result, or review recent questions in the history panel.

The tool selects from 149 maintained prompts rather than writing questions with AI. Choose Conversation, Fun, Thoughtful, Kids, Work, or Writing before generating. It does not provide answer keys, difficulty levels, favorites, or a custom question database. If you have your own reviewed questions, put one per line in the List Randomizer and shuffle them.

What can I use a random question for?

Use a question as an icebreaker, conversation starter, journal prompt, meeting warm-up, interview practice cue, or a way to begin a one-to-one discussion. The list mixes opinions, everyday experiences, preferences, and hypothetical choices, so most prompts do not require specialist knowledge.

A random question works best when it opens a conversation rather than tests the person answering. Ask a follow-up based on what they say instead of immediately generating another prompt. For a group, give everyone a moment to think and make passing acceptable.

How can I use the questions as writing prompts?

Generate one question and answer it for five minutes without editing. If the question is personal, answer through a fictional character instead. Then underline one specific image, conflict, or opinion that could become a longer piece.

For story practice, change the point of view or add a constraint: answer in the voice of someone who cannot tell the truth, set the response ten years in the future, or turn the answer into dialogue. The random question provides a starting direction; it does not need to become the final topic.

How can teachers use a random question?

Preview the result, then use it for a quick-write, pair discussion, speaking exercise, or exit reflection when it supports the lesson. Ask students to use a target vocabulary word, tense, or evidence structure in the response.

The list is not aligned to a grade level or curriculum. Do not generate a prompt publicly and require students to answer before checking it. Avoid questions that ask for sensitive personal disclosure, and provide an alternative prompt or pass option.

What works for meetings and team icebreakers?

Choose prompts that can be answered briefly and do not depend on private information. Preferences, harmless hypotheticals, favorite ways of working, and lessons from ordinary experiences are usually easier than questions about family, health, finances, politics, religion, or workplace complaints.

State how long each answer should take and whether participation is optional. In a short meeting, one shared question with thirty-second answers is often more useful than generating a different question for every person.

What should I check before asking a group?

Read the question before asking it aloud. Consider the participants' ages, relationships, cultural context, accessibility needs, and the purpose of the session. Skip anything too personal, confusing, or unrelated to the activity.

Random selection does not make a prompt automatically neutral or appropriate. A facilitator remains responsible for the question chosen and for allowing people to decline without explanation.

How is recent history handled?

Recent generated questions are stored in this browser so you can see what appeared during the current use and clear the list when finished. The question selection and displayed results are processed by the tool in the browser.
