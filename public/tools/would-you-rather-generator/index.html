How do I use the Would You Rather generator?

Click Generate to select one either-or question from the built-in general-audience list. Read both options, choose one, and explain the reason when you want a longer conversation. You can copy or share the result, generate another question, or review recent results in the history panel.

Choose General, Funny, Kids, or Thoughtful to narrow the 120 built-in questions, or leave All categories selected. The page does not include adult prompts or accept custom questions. Review each result before using it with a particular group.

How do you play Would You Rather?

For two people, take turns answering the same question and ask one follow-up about the reason. For a group, read the question and have participants point left or right, raise a hand, or move to opposite sides of the room. Invite a few voluntary explanations from each side.

Decide before starting whether "neither," "both," or passing is allowed. Requiring a choice can make a playful hypothetical more interesting, but nobody should be pressured to discuss a personal topic.

What makes a good Would You Rather question?

A good question presents two understandable options with a real tradeoff. If one option is obviously better, the conversation ends quickly. Questions become more interesting when each side has a different benefit, inconvenience, ability, setting, or consequence.

The options should still be appropriate for the group. Avoid forcing choices involving harm, discrimination, trauma, sex, health, money, crime, or another person's private information. A light question can be difficult to choose without becoming invasive.

How can teachers use Would You Rather questions?

Preview the result, then use it as a speaking warm-up, opinion paragraph, debate starter, or practice exercise for comparative language. Ask students to choose a side and support it with one or two reasons. A follow-up can require a target connector such as "because," "however," or "on the other hand."

The list is not organized by age, reading level, or curriculum. Provide a pass or alternate question, and do not grade students on which option they choose. Assess the language or reasoning skill instead.

What works for friends, families, and teams?

With friends or family, use questions to uncover preferences and stories rather than to declare a correct answer. Let the discussion continue when a question leads somewhere interesting instead of generating prompts as quickly as possible.

For workplace teams, keep choices neutral and easy to answer in public. Avoid questions about compensation, family status, health, politics, religion, job security, or conflicts with coworkers. The Random Question Generator can provide a broader alternative for meeting warm-ups.

Can people skip or change a question?

Yes. Agree that anyone may pass and that the facilitator may re-roll a question without discussion. This keeps a voluntary game comfortable and avoids making one person's boundary the center of attention.

Questions can repeat because each click is an independent selection from the same list. Use the recent-results panel to notice duplicates and generate another when needed.

Can I use my own Would You Rather questions?

This page does not accept custom prompts. To randomize a list you have written and reviewed, enter one complete question per line in the List Randomizer, shuffle the list, and use the first suitable item.
